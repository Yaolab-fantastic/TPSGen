\abstract{
\textbf{Summary:} Promoter design for tissue-biased expression must account for activity in both target and non-target tissues. TPSGen combines motif-conditioned generation with four-tissue prediction to construct and select tomato promoter candidates. Sequence evidence defines regions to retain, preGAN generates alternatives with fruit-expression guidance, and target scores and margins against non-target tissues guide selection. preGAN retained 99.55\% of nucleotides at conditioned positions in generation experiments. The scoring checkpoint yielded Pearson correlations of 0.78--0.82 across root, stem, leaf and fruit. Four-tissue predictions for 20 sampled preGAN candidates generally favored fruit. TPSGen links each candidate to its retained regions, sequence changes and tissue scores to support selection for experimental testing.\\
\textbf{Availability and Implementation:} TPSGen is implemented in Python as an installable command-line package. Source code, documentation and example workflows are freely available under the MIT license at \url{https://github.com/Yaolab-fantastic/TPSGen}.\\
\textbf{Supplementary information:} Supplementary data will accompany the published article at \textit{Bioinformatics} online.
}

\section{Introduction}

Promoter engineering can tune gene expression and generate useful quantitative trait variation in crops, as demonstrated by promoter editing in tomato \citep{RodriguezLeal2017Engineering}. Studies of minimal and core plant promoters have identified sequence features that influence promoter activity \citep{Cai2020Minimal,Jores2021Synthetic}. Tissue-biased expression introduces a further design requirement: a promoter intended for fruit should favor fruit relative to roots, stems and leaves. Candidate selection must therefore consider both expression strength and its distribution across tissues.

Sequence-to-expression models provide a basis for evaluating regulatory sequences \citep{Washburn2019Evolutionarily}. PhytoExpr extends this approach to the design of regulatory variants and species-specific sequences \citep{Li2024PhytoExpr}. DeepSEED generates flanking sequences around predefined regulatory elements \citep{Zhang2023DeepSEED}, while GPro brings generation, activity prediction and optimization into a common toolkit \citep{Wang2024GPro}. In animal systems, deep-learning methods have also enabled cell-type- and tissue-directed enhancer design \citep{Taskiran2024Enhancers,deAlmeida2024Enhancers,Gosai2024MachineGuided}.

For tomato promoter design, retaining selected sequence regions and identifying tissue preference require complementary steps. Conditional generation uses selected regions as sequence context and encourages their preservation. Cross-tissue prediction then assesses whether a candidate favors the intended tissue. An aggregate expression target, such as the maximum across tissues used by PlantCRE \citep{Qiu2025PlantCRE}, cannot by itself distinguish fruit-biased expression from high expression in several organs. Bringing sequence conditioning and tissue-resolved evaluation into the same workflow allows candidates to be assessed for both retention and tissue preference.

Here, we present TPSGen for motif-conditioned design and selection of tomato promoter candidates. DNABERT-derived sequence evidence defines conditioning templates, preGAN generates candidates with fruit-expression guidance, and TransVAE-MLP predicts scores for root, stem, leaf and fruit. We evaluate retention of the conditioning regions, similarity to tomato promoter sequence distributions and the four-tissue profiles of generated candidates. The software reports candidate sequences together with their source promoters, sequence changes and tissue scores, providing a basis for selecting designs for synthesis and testing.

\section{Implementation}

The model-backed workflow links sequence evidence, conditional generation and four-tissue selection (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). The expression predictors are trained before they are used to guide generation or score candidates. Fruit-expression guidance acts during preGAN training, and the subsequent four-tissue evaluation determines which candidates favor the requested tissue.

\subsection{Promoter data and conditioning regions}

Model development used 165-bp windows immediately upstream of positive-strand gene starts in the ITAG3.2 annotation obtained through Phytozome \citep{Goodstein2012}. Annotated gene starts serve as proxies for transcription start sites. Expression targets came from the MicroTom metabolic network \citep{Li2020}: root, stem and leaf at 85 days post germination (R85, S85 and L85), and fruit at 15 days after breaker (Br15). Values were transformed using $\log(1+x)$ and processed as described in Supplementary Section S1. After sequence--expression matching and preprocessing, the dataset contained 14,071 promoter--target records.

DNABERT was pretrained on 34,725 promoter-related sequences of 500 bp and fine-tuned on 4,448 balanced 165-bp promoter/non-promoter examples \citep{Ji2021}. Attention-derived evidence from overlapping 6-mer tokens was mapped to nucleotide positions. Recurrent patterns in high-evidence segments were used to nominate regions for retention. Each conditioning template contains these retained regions and masks the remaining positions for generation (Supplementary Section S2).

\subsection{Motif-conditioned generation}

preGAN generates a complete sequence from a 165-bp masked template. A linear projection and three one-dimensional convolutional residual blocks produce nucleotide probabilities. A conditional critic compares template--reference and template--generated pairs. Training used 5,100 paired promoters selected for high Br15-associated expression, with 4,080 records used for generator updates.

The generator objective combines an adversarial loss, an $L_1$ condition-consistency penalty and a fruit-expression prediction loss. The consistency term encourages agreement with the supplied template. A separately trained, frozen DenseNet--LSTM predictor supplies the fruit score, and the prediction loss measures its squared deviation from the target value. Generated candidates are then evaluated by TransVAE-MLP across all four tissues. Supplementary Section S3 gives the encoding, objective and training settings.

\subsection{Four-tissue scoring and selection}

TransVAE-MLP learns sequence reconstruction and four-target regression. A Transformer encoder and convolutional bottleneck parameterize a latent distribution. During training, a decoder reconstructs the sequence and a multilayer perceptron (MLP) predicts the four processed expression targets. The objective combines reconstruction, latent regularization, expression regression and a 3-mer composition term. At inference, the MLP uses the latent mean to produce one four-score profile per sequence (Supplementary Section S4).

For a candidate sequence $x$ and target tissue $t$, the target-bias margin is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t(x)=s_t(x)-\max_{j\ne t}s_j(x),
\label{eq:target-bias}
\end{equation}
where $s_j(x)$ is the score for tissue $j$ and the maximum is over the
three non-target tissues. A candidate is eligible for strict selection
only if the requested tissue has the highest score, its target-bias
margin is positive, and its profile concentration meets the default
threshold ($\tau\geq0.7$). Sequence quality and agreement at retained
positions are also required. Eligible candidates are ranked within each
input first by target-bias margin and then by target score.

If strict preGAN selection yields no candidates, the workflow can search positions marked editable in the template and reapply the selection criteria. An optional adaptive mode can return positive-margin candidates at a lower effective $\tau$ threshold. These candidates are labelled \emph{target-biased}, and the requested and effective thresholds are recorded separately (Supplementary Sections S4.3--S4.4).

\subsection{Software inputs and outputs}

TPSGen accepts multi-record FASTA files and checks sequence length and the A/C/G/T alphabet. Generation and scoring backends can be selected separately. A package-native route provides exact-pattern scanning, seeded substitutions and deterministic scoring for the software demonstration. Each run applies the same scorer to input and candidate sequences and reports retained regions, sequence changes, four-tissue scores and target margins. Candidate FASTA files and run metadata support downstream selection and reproducible execution (Supplementary Section S5).

\section{Evaluation of promoter design and selection}

Four-tissue prediction provides the basis for comparing candidate expression profiles. On the 3,703-record held-out validation cohort used for checkpoint characterization, the TransVAE-MLP scoring checkpoint yielded Pearson correlations of 0.78, 0.82, 0.80 and 0.79 for root, stem, leaf and fruit, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:main-results}C,D). These values are not presented as an independent external evaluation. The separate model-development test results are reported in Supplementary Section S4.2.

The sequence-evidence analysis identified regions for conditional generation. DNABERT achieved an F1 score of 0.906 and a Matthews correlation coefficient of 0.813 on promoter classification. Positional evidence was concentrated approximately 50--30 bp upstream of the gene-start proxy, and perturbation of selected regions increased model loss (Supplementary Fig.~S1). In the preGAN evaluation, 99.55\% of nucleotides at non-masked positions matched the reference. Generated-versus-natural $k$-mer correlations were 0.95, 0.93 and 0.89 for $k=4,5,6$, compared with 0.91, 0.88 and 0.86 for WGAN. These results characterize retention of the conditioning regions and agreement with tomato promoter sequence distributions (Supplementary Table S3 and Fig.~S3A).

To examine tissue preference after generation, 20 preGAN candidates were sampled and scored across the four targets. Their predicted profiles generally favored Br15 (Supplementary Fig.~S2C,D). Comparing each fruit score with the three non-target scores identifies candidates with predicted fruit preference.

The package-native demonstration processed 40 promoters and generated three candidates per input, yielding 120 sequences. Their top-ranked candidates showed upward shifts in the displayed
fruit-score and bias-margin distributions relative to the inputs under
the package-native scorer (Fig.~\ref{fig:main-results}A,B). Sequence logos and frequency changes summarize the corresponding edits (Fig.~\ref{fig:main-results}E). This example illustrates the software's input-to-candidate workflow and comparison outputs.

\section{Conclusion}

TPSGen couples motif-conditioned generation with four-tissue prediction to select tomato promoter candidates for tissue-biased expression. preGAN retained 99.55\% of nucleotides at conditioned positions, and the four-tissue predictions for 20 sampled preGAN candidates generally favored fruit. By reporting sequence changes alongside target and non-target scores, TPSGen supports selection of promoter candidates for synthesis and experimental testing.
