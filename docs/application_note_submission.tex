\abstract{
\textbf{Summary:} Designing promoters for tissue-biased expression requires comparing activity across target and non-target tissues. TPSGen combines motif-conditioned candidate construction with four-tissue scoring for tomato promoter design. Sequence evidence defines the conditioning templates, and target-tissue scores and bias margins guide candidate prioritization. In model-development experiments, preGAN retained 99.55\% of nucleotides at conditioned positions. Evaluation of the released scoring checkpoint yielded Pearson correlations of 0.78--0.82 across root, stem, leaf and fruit. TPSGen provides candidate sequences, motif annotations and tissue-score profiles for experimental evaluation.\\
\textbf{Availability and Implementation:} TPSGen is implemented in Python and distributed as an installable command-line package. Source code, documentation and example workflows are freely available under the MIT license at \url{https://github.com/Yaolab-fantastic/TPSGen}.\\

\section{Introduction}

Engineering promoter sequences can tune gene expression and generate useful
quantitative trait variation in crops, as demonstrated by promoter editing in
tomato \citep{RodriguezLeal2017Engineering}. Studies of minimal and core plant
promoters have identified sequence features that support the design of
promoters with different activities \citep{Cai2020Minimal,Jores2021Synthetic}.
For tissue-directed applications, promoter design must account for both
expression strength and its distribution across tissues. A promoter intended
for fruit-focused expression should favor fruit relative to roots, stems and leaves.

Sequence-to-expression models have enabled prediction of plant transcript
abundance from regulatory DNA \citep{Washburn2019Evolutionarily}. PhytoExpr
extends this approach to computational searches for functional regulatory
variants and species-specific regulatory sequences \citep{Li2024PhytoExpr}.
DeepSEED generates flanking sequences conditioned on predefined cis-regulatory
elements, retaining selected regulatory sequences during design
\citep{Zhang2023DeepSEED}. GPro combines generative models, activity predictors
and optimization methods within a common toolkit \citep{Wang2024GPro}.
Deep-learning approaches have also enabled cell-type- and tissue-directed
enhancer design in animal systems
\citep{Taskiran2024Enhancers,deAlmeida2024Enhancers,Gosai2024MachineGuided}.

However, a single expression value does not show which tissue a promoter
favors. PlantCRE, for
example, predicts the maximum expression across tissues rather than a
tissue-resolved profile \citep{Qiu2025Plasticity}. Such a summary cannot
distinguish a fruit-biased candidate from one predicted to be active across
several organs. For tomato promoter design, candidates therefore need to
be evaluated across the target and non-target tissues. Motif-conditioned
construction retains selected regulatory regions during sequence
modification, and cross-tissue scoring allows the resulting candidates to
be prioritized for target-tissue activity and relative tissue preference.

Here, we present TPSGen, a Python framework for motif-conditioned design of
tomato promoter candidates for tissue-biased expression. It converts
sequence evidence into conditioning templates, generates candidates with
fruit-associated guidance and evaluates them across root, stem, leaf and
fruit. Target-tissue scores and target-bias margins guide candidate
prioritization. We assess the sequence evidence, retention of conditioned
positions and four-tissue profiles of generated candidates, and provide a
40-promoter example of the package-native workflow. Candidate sequences
are linked to their source promoters, sequence changes and score profiles
for comparison and experimental selection.

\section{Implementation}

TPSGen provides a model-backed workflow linking DNABERT sequence evidence,
preGAN generation and TransVAE-MLP scoring (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). Its
package-native route uses exact-pattern scanning, seeded sequence
substitutions and a deterministic scorer. Generation and scoring backends
are selected separately, and the same scorer is applied to input and
candidate sequences. Execution settings and commands are provided in
Supplementary Section S5.

\subsection{Promoter data and sequence evidence}

Inputs are 165-bp tomato promoter sequences in multi-record FASTA format.
For model development, windows were extracted immediately upstream of
positive-strand gene starts in the ITAG3.2 annotation obtained through
Phytozome \citep{Goodstein2012PhytozomeSource}. Annotated gene starts serve
as proxies for transcription start sites. TPSGen checks sequence length
and the A/C/G/T alphabet before candidate construction.

Expression targets were obtained from the MicroTom metabolic network
\citep{Li2020MMNSource}. Promoter sequences were matched to gene expression
in root, stem and leaf at 85 days post germination (R85, S85 and L85), and
fruit at 15 days after breaker (Br15). The four columns were transformed
using $\log(1+x)$ and processed as described in Supplementary Section S1.
The resulting ITAG-MMNP2E resource contains 14,071 promoter--target records.
These four organ--stage measurements define the model-backed scoring targets.

DNABERT was pretrained on 34,725 promoter-related sequences of 500 bp and
fine-tuned on 4,448 balanced 165-bp promoter/non-promoter examples
\citep{Ji2021}. Attention-derived evidence from overlapping 6-mer tokens
is projected onto nucleotide positions. High-evidence segments are
collected into recurrent sequence patterns and used to nominate regions
for retention during generation. The package-native scanner instead
identifies exact matches to configured patterns. The two evidence sources
are recorded separately (Supplementary Section S2).

\subsection{Motif-conditioned candidate generation}

The package-native generator leaves the selected motif positions unchanged
and applies seeded substitutions at the remaining positions. The preGAN
route uses a 165-bp template in which high-evidence regions retain their
sequence and editable positions are masked. Its generator uses a linear
projection followed by three one-dimensional convolutional residual blocks
to output nucleotide probabilities. A conditional critic compares template--reference and
template--generated sequence pairs.

preGAN was trained on 5,100 paired tomato promoter records selected for
high Br15-associated expression. Its objective combines an adversarial
loss, an $L_1$ condition-consistency penalty and a fruit-score
prediction loss. The $L_1$ term encourages retention of the supplied
sequence context. A frozen DenseNet--LSTM predictor supplies the fruit
score, and the prediction loss measures its squared deviation from the
target value. The released workflow additionally checks retained positions
and applies constrained four-tissue-guided search when strict preGAN candidates
are unavailable. After generation, TransVAE-MLP evaluates the candidate across
all four tissues (Supplementary Section S3).

\subsection{Four-tissue scoring and candidate selection}

TransVAE-MLP jointly learns sequence reconstruction and four-target
regression. A Transformer encoder and convolutional bottleneck parameterize
a latent distribution. During training, a decoder reconstructs the sequence,
while a multilayer perceptron (MLP) predicts the four processed MMN targets.
The loss combines reconstruction, latent-distribution regularization,
expression regression and a 3-mer composition term. At inference, the MLP
uses the latent mean to produce one four-score profile per sequence
(Supplementary Section S4).

For target tissue $t$, the target-bias margin is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t(x)=s_t(x)-\max_{j\ne t}s_j(x),
\label{eq:target-bias}
\end{equation}
where $s_t(x)$ is the target-tissue score and the maximum is taken over
the other three tissues. A positive margin indicates that the target has
the highest score. Candidates are ranked within each input by target-tissue
score, with larger margins used to resolve ties. The complementary index
$\tau$ summarizes the concentration of the four-score profile
(Supplementary Section S1.4). The released workflow first applies the
paper-level threshold of $\tau\geq0.7$. If no candidate meets this threshold,
an optional adaptive fallback may return a target-highest, positive-margin
candidate labelled \emph{target-biased}; the requested and effective
thresholds are both recorded, and such a result is not labelled strongly
tissue-specific. Scores are compared within the selected backend.

Output tables link each candidate to its source promoter, sequence
changes, retained regions, four-tissue scores and target-bias margin.
Original-sequence scores are included for comparison. Candidate FASTA
files support sequence selection for synthesis, while run metadata record
the generation and scoring backends, checkpoint use and random seed.

\section{Sequence evidence, generation and candidate evaluation}

DNABERT achieved an F1 score of 0.906 and a Matthews correlation coefficient
of 0.813 on the promoter-classification test set (Supplementary Table S2).
Attention-derived evidence was concentrated approximately 50--30 bp upstream
of the gene-start proxy, and recurrent high-evidence patterns included
CTATT, ACTAA, ATCCA and TTTAT. Perturbation of selected regions increased
model loss in the in-silico mutagenesis analysis (Supplementary Fig.~S1).

In the preGAN model-development evaluation, 99.55\% of nucleotides at
non-masked positions matched the reference sequence, quantifying retention
of the conditioning context. In the model comparison, generated-versus-natural
$k$-mer frequency correlations were 0.95, 0.93 and 0.89 for $k=4,5,6$,
compared with 0.91, 0.88 and 0.86 for WGAN (Supplementary Table S3 and
Fig.~S3A).

Twenty preGAN candidates were also scored with TransVAE-MLP. Their
four-score profiles generally favored Br15, and 18 had $\tau>0.7$, with
$\tau$ ranging from 0.658 to 0.879 across the set (Supplementary Fig.~S2C,D).


For the released TransVAE-MLP checkpoint, correlations between predicted
scores and reference expression labels were 0.78, 0.82, 0.80 and 0.79 for
root, stem, leaf and fruit, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:main-results}C).
The fruit analysis used 3,703 checkpoint-evaluation records and yielded
Pearson $r=0.79$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:main-results}D). The model-development test results
are reported in Supplementary Section S4.2. This checkpoint-characterization
cohort is not presented as an independent external performance evaluation.

Using fruit as the target, we also ran the package-native workflow on
40 promoters and generated three candidates per input, yielding 120
sequences. Compared with the inputs, their Rank-1 candidates showed higher
fruit-associated score and bias-margin distributions under the native
scorer (Fig.~\ref{fig:main-results}A,B). Figure~\ref{fig:main-results}E shows
the corresponding sequence logos and position-wise frequency changes.

\section{Conclusion}

TPSGen connects sequence evidence, motif-conditioned generation and
four-tissue evaluation for tomato promoter design. It supports candidate
selection by showing which sequence regions were retained and how the
resulting score profile favors the target tissue. The software provides
ranked sequences and their associated evidence for experimental testing.
